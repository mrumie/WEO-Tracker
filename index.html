Key Theme of this Report
War shock meets AI tech boom
Two opposing forces: the Middle East war (negative supply shock) partly offset by an accelerating AI-driven technology cycle. Impact varies sharply by a country's war exposure and position in the tech value chain.
Key Variables
Growth Steady, uneven Global growth 3,0% in 2026 (–0,1pp vs Apr), then 3,4% in 2027 (+0,2pp) — broadly unchanged cumulatively. The war drag is offset by AI-tech momentum. Q1 2026 surprised on the upside (3,0% vs 2,7% forecast), led by AI-hardware exporters (Korea +7,5%, China +8,1%, Japan +1,8%, Germany +1,4%).
Inflation Disinflation stalled Global headline inflation revised up to 4,7% in 2026 (+0,3pp vs Apr), easing to 3,9% in 2027 (+0,2pp). The disinflation trend since early 2024 has stalled, driven by higher energy and food prices. Core inflation stable so far; expectations for 2026 rose but 2027 much less.
Financial Conditions Eased, accommodative Conditions eased since the early-April peak and remain accommodative by historical standards. Corporate spreads historically tight; equities strong (80%+ of S&P 500 beat Q1 earnings). Markets price higher policy rates as inflation reappears; long-term yields up. AI-stock concentration keeps intensifying.
Key Structural Issue Two opposing shocks The update frames the world as caught in crosscurrents of war and technology. Energy exporters outside the conflict gain from favorable terms of trade; economies plugged into the AI-led upturn do well even as energy importers; but energy importers outside the tech chain (many low-income countries) are squeezed on both sides.
Risk Balance
  • ▶ Renewed Middle East conflict could reignite commodity-price volatility, supply shortages and FX pressures
  • ▶ Oil inventories are near multiyear lows, so a fresh disruption could move prices non-linearly
  • ▶ A correction in AI/tech expectations could sharply reprice frothy equity valuations and tighten conditions
  • ▶ Eroded fiscal buffers leave high-debt sovereigns exposed to a repricing of debt sustainability
  • ▶ On the upside, a smoother-than-assumed Hormuz reopening would lower prices and lift growth
  • ▶ Stronger AI investment and durable trade cooperation could push growth above baseline

Key Quote
"Global economic activity and the outlook are being shaped by two major forces, pushing in opposite directions with asymmetric effects across countries. First is the negative supply shock induced by the war in the Middle East. Second is the ongoing positive technology shock manifesting in accelerated momentum of the global technology cycle." — IMF WEO Update, July 2026 · "Global Economy in Crosscurrents of War and Technology"
Investment Implications
AI-hardware exporters — momentum play
Korea, Taiwan, Malaysia, Thailand surprised strongly on semiconductor/AI-hardware exports. The tech cycle is the dominant tailwind, outweighing war exposure for these economies.
Energy — elevated but off the peak
Oil ~$89/bbl assumed for 2026 (+32% vs 2025), but ceasefire and Iran-US MOU cooled prices from the April peak. Inventories near multiyear lows keep upside risk alive if conflict reignites.
Energy importers outside tech chain — underweight
Low-income and commodity-importing economies without AI-chain participation face the worst mix: higher energy/food prices, FX and BoP stress, and no tech offset.
Rates — higher for longer
Disinflation has stalled; central banks are pricing higher nominal rates and holding real rates steady. Long yields up. Watch second-round effects and any de-anchoring of expectations.

Key Theme of this Report
Middle East war shock + energy disruption
New military conflict in Middle East (since Feb 2026) disrupting Strait of Hormuz. Energy price spike, inflation rebound, financial tightening. Comes on top of still-unresolved trade fragmentation from 2025.
Key Variables
Growth Decelerating World GDP 2026 unchanged vs Oct '25 at the aggregate level (both 3,1%), but with significant regional divergence. Absent the war, the IMF would have revised upward. US: 2,3% (Oct '25: 2,0%). Euro area: 1,1% (Oct '25: 1,2%). Middle East hardest hit: revision of nearly –3pp — most affected by the conflict and the disruption to the Strait of Hormuz.
Inflation Re-accelerating Global inflation rises to 4,4% in 2026 (Oct '25: ~4,1%), reversing the disinflation trend. The energy shock from the conflict is the main driver. Commodity-importing EMs with preexisting vulnerabilities face double pressure: higher prices + currency depreciation. Inflation is projected to resume its decline to 3,7% in 2027.
Financial Conditions Moderately tighter Moderate tightening of financial conditions post-conflict. The USD strengthened (safe haven). Bond yields rose; equities fell. EM spreads widening, especially for commodity importers. Even so, conditions remain accommodative vs. historical standards per the April 2026 GFSR.
Key Structural Issue Defense spending boom The WEO's analytical chapter examines the macroeconomic consequences of rising defense budgets. A typical defense boom raises spending by about 2,7pp of GDP, mostly financed by borrowing, which pushes public debt up by roughly 7pp over three years (and far more in wartime). The short-term growth boost is real but modest, and the main concern is the trade-off: higher defense outlays can crowd out social spending and strain fiscal sustainability over the medium term.
Risk Balance
  • ▶ If the Middle East conflict escalates beyond the reference scenario, energy supply could be severely disrupted
  • ▶ A persistent energy price shock could trigger second-round inflation, forcing central banks to tighten further
  • ▶ Lingering trade fragmentation, together with the USMCA review due in July 2026, could reignite tensions
  • ▶ In high-debt countries, fiscal vulnerabilities could worsen as higher interest costs strain budgets
  • ▶ On the upside, the AI productivity dividend could materialize faster than expected, lifting growth
  • ▶ Alternatively, a short-lived conflict and ceasefire could quickly restore confidence and supply chains

Key Quote
"Over the past year, headwinds from higher trade barriers and elevated uncertainty have been offset by tailwinds from technology-related investment and fiscal and monetary policy support. The Middle East conflict presents a significant counterforce to these tailwinds through its impact on commodity markets, inflation expectations, and financial conditions." — IMF World Economic Outlook, April 2026 · Executive Summary
Investment Implications
Energy sector — tactical overweight
Energy price spike and supply disruption through Strait of Hormuz supports oil & gas producers. Tactical overweight in energy equities and commodities while conflict persists.
EM commodity importers — underweight
Countries combining high energy import dependency, USD debt, and thin policy buffers face triple pressure. MENA, South Asia, frontier Africa most exposed. Spread widening expected.
Latam — selective; Chile resilient
Copper exporters (Chile) benefit from supply chain reorientation. Mexico faces USMCA review risk. Brazil domestic demand relatively insulated but fiscal consolidation a constraint.
Defense sector equities — structural tailwind
IMF confirms defense spending boom is structural and multi-year. European defense budgets rising sharply. Long-term positioning in defense industrial complex supported.
